FAQ

Questions, answered.

A cloud phone is a full Android device running on remote ARM hardware in a data center. You get the complete OS — not an emulator or a container — streamed to your browser or controlled through an API. It behaves like a physical phone in every way that matters to an app.

Emulators run x86 instructions translated to look like ARM, and most apps can detect them. NimbusFarm devices run native ARM64 on real mobile silicon with genuine hardware identifiers, so detection systems see a normal phone.

A single device is ready in about 8 seconds. A batch of 500 devices typically finishes provisioning in under 90 seconds, and you can watch each one come online in real time on your dashboard.

Yes. Each instance is a separate virtual machine with its own kernel, storage, IMEI, MAC address, Android ID, advertising ID, and build fingerprint. There is no shared state between devices on your fleet or anyone else's.

Absolutely. Upload APKs through the dashboard or API, push files over ADB, and run any automation framework you like — Appium, UiAutomator, Espresso, or your own tooling. Root access is available on request.

Billing is per device-minute while a device is running. Stopped devices cost nothing and keep their full state, so you can spin up 500 phones for a launch week and pause them the moment you're done.
